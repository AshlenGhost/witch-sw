How to play

A hidden-role card game for 4–12 players around one table. The town wins when every Witch trial card has been revealed. The coven wins when everyone left alive holds a Witch trial card.

Trial cards

Each player is dealt trial cards face down on the table in front of them: mostly Innocent, plus one or two Witch cards and one Constable. You can look at your own. Hold a Witch card and you are part of the coven. When a Witch card is turned face up, its holder dies. When all of a player’s trial cards are face up, they die too.

Before the first night

After the deal, everyone arranges their trial cards face down in any order they like. Accusers pick cards by position, so where you hide your Witch or Constable matters.

Your turn

Do one of two things: draw 2 cards, or play any number of cards from your hand and then end your turn. No card from your hand can be played on yourself. You can play several copies of an accusation or Defense card on the same player in one go.

  • Red cards are accusations (1, 3 or 7 points). When a player reaches 7 points (or 14 if they have Devotion), they go on trial. Whoever played the last accusation picks one of their face-down trial cards to turn over, and their accusations are cleared.
  • Green cards take effect at once: remove accusations, skip a turn, burn a hand, steal a hand, or shift blame.
  • Blue cards lie face up on the table in front of a player: Sanctuary (safe at night), Devotion (needs 14 accusation points to go on trial instead of 7), Bound Souls (the two holders are linked: one dies, both die).

Night Falls

Drawing this card starts a night. The coven secretly votes on a victim (30 seconds; most votes wins, a tie is decided at random between the tied choices), then the Constable guards someone (not themselves). Who the Constable protects is announced to everyone for 10 seconds, so that player knows they don’t need to confess. A Constable whose card has been revealed can no longer protect anyone. At dawn, anyone may confess by turning over one of their own trial cards. The marked player dies unless they were guarded, confessed, or are in Sanctuary. Dawn announces who the Constable protected, who the coven attacked, and what happened. When the night ends, the discard pile is shuffled back into the deck, and Night Falls always goes among the last 10 cards.

The Pact

The Raven is a blue card the coven places face up in front of any player on the first night (even a witch, themselves included). After that it acts like any other blue card: Hex Breaker can discard it, Blame moves it, and once discarded it can be drawn and played again. When The Pact is drawn, the player who drew it turns over one trial card of whoever holds the Raven. Then every living player chooses one face-down trial card (by its position) to take from the player on their left. A villager who takes a Witch card joins the coven, and once a witch, always a witch: even if your Witch card is later taken, you stay in the coven. The town still wins once every Witch card has been revealed. When a player dies, everyone learns whether they were a witch. A dead witch with no Witch card among their trial cards must have passed it on in the Pact, so it is now in someone else’s hands.
